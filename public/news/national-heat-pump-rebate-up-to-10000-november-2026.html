National Heat Pump Rebate: up to $10,000, and installs from November 1 can count

By Calcitax Advisors · November 14, 2026

The federal government has announced a new National Heat Pump Rebate worth $2,000 for most homeowners and up to $10,000 for households below the median income. The online application portal will not open until early 2027, but heat pumps installed on or after November 1, 2026 may qualify, as long as you apply once the program opens. If you are replacing your heating this winter, keep every receipt.

What happened

On October 8, 2026, Prime Minister Mark Carney announced $2 billion over eight years for home energy retrofits. The centrepiece is the National Heat Pump Rebate, which aims to help up to 820,000 households switch to efficient electric heat pumps. A heat pump is an electric system that moves heat into your home in winter and out of it in summer, so it heats and cools.

Environment and Climate Change Canada has published the main rules. Some details are still being finalized and could change; your eligibility will be judged on the rules in force on the date your heat pump is installed.

How much you could get

  • $2,000 for households above the median income.
  • Up to $10,000 for households at or below the median income. The exact amount depends on your income and what the equipment costs.

"Income" here means household after-tax income from your 2025 tax return: your net income (line 23600) minus your total taxes payable (line 43500), plus the same figure for your spouse or common-law partner. You compare that to the median for your province and household size. For British Columbia, the thresholds are:

To get the higher amount, you will give your Social Insurance Number when you apply, and the CRA will check your income. That means your 2025 return needs to be filed. If you haven't filed it yet, do it before the portal opens.

Who can apply

  • You must live in Canada and own the home. It doesn't have to be where you live, so a rental property can qualify.
  • Only one rebate per household, even if you own several properties.
  • The address must not have received federal funding for a heat pump before.
  • Your current heating can be electric (baseboards or an electric furnace), natural gas, oil, propane, diesel, coal or wood.
  • Both planned replacements and emergency replacements count. You don't need to prove your old system broke down, and you don't have to remove it.
  • No energy audit is needed before or after the installation.

What the heat pump must meet

  • It must be on the federal eligible product list, identified by its AHRI number (a certification number the contractor can give you).
  • It must be able to heat your whole home. Ask your contractor to confirm the sizing.
  • It must be bought from and installed by an HVAC contractor.

Keep the right receipts

You will have six months to submit receipts. The contractor's receipt must show:

  • your name and address,
  • the installing company's name,
  • the make, model and AHRI number of the equipment,
  • how many heat pumps were installed, and
  • the total project cost, including related work such as ductwork and electrical upgrades.

Timing

  • November 1, 2026: installations from this date may qualify.
  • Early 2027: the online portal opens.
  • March 2034: the program is scheduled to close.

No money is paid until the program launches and your application is approved. The government says funding is not guaranteed until approval.

What about B.C. rebates?

B.C. homeowners already have provincial options. For homes heated with electricity, the CleanBC Better Homes and Home Renovation Rebate Program, run with BC Hydro and FortisBC, currently lists up to $4,000 for a whole-home heat pump and $1,500 for a partial-home heat pump, using a contractor from the Home Performance Contractor Network. Higher, income-qualified rebates are offered through the CleanBC Energy Savings Program, and homes heated with natural gas, propane or oil have separate rebates on the Better Homes BC website.

If you installed a heat pump under BC Hydro's bonus promotion (installations from August 1 to October 31, 2026), the application deadline is November 30, 2026.

The Prime Minister's announcement says the new federal rebate can be combined with provincial programs, but the federal program page doesn't yet spell out how. Check both programs' rules before you sign a contract, and confirm your contractor qualifies for each.

A tax note for landlords

If the heat pump goes into a rental property, it is usually a capital cost, claimed over time as capital cost allowance (CCA, which is tax depreciation). The CRA says that when you receive a government grant or rebate to buy depreciable property, you subtract it from the property's capital cost. For your own home, the rebate simply lowers what you pay.

Watch for scams

The government does not send door-to-door salespeople or make marketing calls about heating equipment. Choose a qualified HVAC company yourself.

What to do now

  • Sign up for program updates on the federal National Heat Pump Rebate page.
  • Make sure your 2025 tax return (and your spouse's) is filed.
  • Before buying, check the model is on the eligible product list and that it can heat your whole home.
  • Get an itemized invoice showing the AHRI number.
  • Check B.C. rebates on Better Homes BC or with a CleanBC energy coach.
