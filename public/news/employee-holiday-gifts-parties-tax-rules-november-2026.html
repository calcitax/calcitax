Holiday gifts and parties for staff: how to keep them tax-free in 2026

By Calcitax Advisors · November 5, 2026

Many small businesses thank their staff in December with a gift, a gift card or a party. The Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) lets you do this tax-free for employees, but only if you stay within a few clear limits. Here is what to check before you buy or book.

Why this matters now

The rules haven't changed, but this is the season when they matter most. The CRA's policies on gifts, awards and social events are set out on its payroll pages, last updated in 2025 and 2026. A taxable benefit is something of value an employer gives an employee that has to be added to the employee's income on their T4 slip. If you stay inside the CRA's limits, a gift or party is not a taxable benefit and you have nothing extra to report.

Separately, the federal government is tabling Budget 2026 on November 5, 2026. We will cover any changes it announces in a separate article.

Gifts: the $500 rule

  • Non-cash gifts and awards are tax-free if their total value, including taxes, is $500 or less per employee for the year. There is no limit on the number of items.
  • Anything above $500 is taxable. For example, if you give an employee gifts worth $650 in a year, $150 is a taxable benefit.
  • Small items such as coffee, T-shirts, mugs, plaques and trophies don't count toward the $500.
  • Cash and near-cash gifts are always taxable, whatever the amount. Near-cash means things that work like cash, such as securities, precious metals and gift cards that don't meet the CRA's conditions.
  • A reward for job performance, such as hitting a sales target, is always taxable. The $500 rule covers gifts for special occasions (such as a holiday or birthday) and awards for overall contribution to the workplace.

Gift cards: three conditions

A gift card counts as a non-cash gift (and can fit inside the $500 limit) only if all of these are true:

  • it can only be used at one store, or at a group of stores named on the card,
  • its terms say the balance can't be turned into cash, and
  • you keep a log showing the employee's name, the date, the reason, the type of card, the amount and the store or stores.

A general prepaid card that works almost anywhere may not qualify. If a card fails these tests, it is treated as near-cash and is fully taxable.

Holiday parties: the $150 rule

An in-person holiday party or other social event is not a taxable benefit if:

  • it is open to all employees (a whole branch or division counts),
  • it costs $150 or less per person, including taxes, where spouses and common-law partners who attend count as persons, and
  • it is within the limit of six employer-paid social events a year.

Taxi rides home and overnight hotel rooms for people attending are left out of the $150 count. If the cost goes over $150 per person, the whole amount becomes taxable, not just the part above $150. The CRA gives a simple example: a party costing $16,500 for 100 people works out to $165 per person, so each employee has a $165 benefit (or $330 if their spouse also came).

For virtual events, the CRA's limit is $50 per employee for meals, drinks and delivery, or $100 if entertainment is included.

What it means for small businesses

  • Owners and family are different. Gifts to relatives, shareholders and other people who aren't at arm's length are outside the CRA's gift policy, so their full value is taxable.
  • Your deduction. Normally only 50% of food, drink and entertainment costs can be deducted. The Income Tax Act makes an exception for up to six special events in a calendar year where the food or entertainment is generally available to all employees at a place of business.
  • If a gift is taxable, you must add it to the employee's pay for that period, withhold income tax and CPP, and report it on the T4. For cash gifts, you also deduct EI.

What to do now

  • Add up what each employee has already received this year before buying more gifts.
  • Choose physical gifts or store-specific gift cards instead of cash or general prepaid cards.
  • Start a gift card log now, with all six details the CRA asks for.
  • Budget your party at $150 per person or less, including taxes and partners, and invite everyone.
  • Keep receipts and a guest count so you can show the cost per person if asked.

A little planning means your team gets the full value of your thank-you, without a surprise on their T4.
