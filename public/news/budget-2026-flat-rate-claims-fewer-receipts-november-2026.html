Budget 2026 proposes flat-rate claims for moving, medical and other costs: fewer receipts

By Calcitax Advisors · November 16, 2026

The federal budget tabled on November 5, 2026 proposes letting people claim a fixed, flat amount for several deductions and credits instead of adding up every receipt. Examples include $1,000 for a work-related move, $1,000 for gluten-free food and $2,000 for a service dog's costs. These are proposals: they need Parliament to pass them before they become law.

Our budget overview covered the headline measures. This article looks at the simpler claim options, which got less attention but could save many households time at tax time.

What happened

Today, to claim most deductions and credits you total your actual costs and keep receipts in case the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) asks to see them. Finance Canada says the new options are meant to reduce the need to keep receipts and itemize expenses. In each case the flat amount is optional: you can still claim your actual costs if they are higher, but you generally can't do both for the same expense.

Flat amounts proposed from the 2027 tax year

  • Moving expenses: $1,000. If you move for a new job or business location (today's rule: your new home must be at least 40 km closer to the new work location), you could deduct a flat $1,000 instead of itemizing. As now, the deduction would be limited to income you earn at the new location (or, for students, eligible scholarship income). Spouses can't both claim the same move.
  • Home Accessibility Tax Credit: $1,000. This credit applies to qualifying home renovations. Today you can claim up to $20,000 of actual expenses a year. The flat $1,000 option could be used only once in a person's lifetime, and only one person could use it for a home in a given year.
  • Adoption expenses: $1,200 per child, claimed in the year the adoption is completed. (The current maximum for actual expenses is $19,972 for 2026 for a child under 18.)
  • Labour mobility deduction for tradespeople: $1,500 a year, instead of claiming actual eligible expenses (the deduction is up to $10,000 in 2026).
  • Clergy residence deduction: $1,000 for each eligible month, up to $12,000 a year.

Medical expense flat amounts

The medical expense tax credit (METC) is a credit for eligible medical costs. The budget proposes three optional flat amounts:

  • Gluten-free food: $1,000 a year, starting with the 2026 tax year. Today, people with celiac disease must work out the extra cost of gluten-free products compared with regular ones, item by item. The flat amount removes that calculation.
  • Incontinence products: $500 a year, from 2027.
  • Service dog: $2,000 a year for care costs, from 2027. The cost of acquiring the dog could still be claimed separately. The option would not cover service animals other than dogs.

Fewer prescriptions needed

Also starting with the 2026 tax year, some medical devices would no longer need a prescription to qualify, including insulin, CPAP machines, infusion pumps for diabetes and blood coagulation monitors. For certain other items, a prescription would be replaced by a medical practitioner certifying your condition. The CRA could still ask for more documents.

What it means for you

  • Less paperwork for small claims. If your real costs are modest or hard to prove, the flat amount may be simpler and close to what you would claim anyway.
  • Bigger costs still pay to itemize. A long-distance move or a large accessibility renovation will often cost far more than $1,000. In those cases keep your receipts and claim actual costs.
  • Watch the 2026 items. The gluten-free flat amount and the prescription changes are proposed to apply to your 2026 return, which you'll file next spring. Whether they're available will depend on the legislation passing in time.
  • Couples and families: only one person can use a given flat amount for the same move, home, child or patient, so decide together who claims it.

What it means for small businesses

The labour mobility option may help tradespeople who travel to job sites, and the moving option may help self-employed people who relocate their business. Business expenses claimed on your T2125 statement are not affected: those still need normal records. Our guide to self-employed expenses and record-keeping guide explain what to keep.

What to do now

  • Keep receipts for 2026 as usual. None of these options is law yet, so your current records are your safety net.
  • If you buy gluten-free food for celiac disease, keep your grocery receipts for 2026 anyway, in case the flat amount isn't in place by filing season.
  • Planning an accessibility renovation or a move in 2027? Compare your likely costs with the flat amount before you decide how to claim.
  • See our guide to often-missed tax credits for credits you may already qualify for.

We'll report when the budget legislation passes or the CRA publishes details.
