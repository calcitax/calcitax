Buying a new home? First-time buyers can get back up to $50,000 of GST

By Calcitax Advisors · November 20, 2026

If you are a first-time buyer purchasing a newly built home in B.C., you may be able to get back all of the GST on it, up to $50,000. The First-Time Home Buyers' (FTHB) GST/HST rebate became law on March 12, 2026, and the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) is now taking applications, including for homes that closed before the law passed.

What happened

The rebate was first announced in 2025. Parliament passed it in Bill C-4, which received Royal Assent (final approval) on March 12, 2026, and it applies back to agreements signed on or after March 20, 2025. In October 2026 the CRA released an updated version of its new housing rebate guide (RC4028) that includes the FTHB rules. Federal Budget 2026, tabled on November 5, 2026, lists the rebate as an existing support and does not change it.

In B.C. there is no HST, so the rebate covers the 5% GST you pay on a new home.

Who qualifies

  • You are at least 18 and a Canadian citizen or permanent resident.
  • You are a first-time buyer: in the current year and the previous four calendar years, you did not live as your main home in a place owned by you or your spouse or common-law partner, in Canada or abroad.
  • Neither you nor your spouse or partner has claimed this rebate before.
  • The home will be your primary place of residence, and you are the first person to live in it after it is finished. Cottages and investment properties don't qualify.

The CRA gives a simple example: someone who sold their home in June 2022 is not a first-time buyer for a purchase that closes in August 2026, but would be for one that closes in February 2027.

If several people buy together, at least one owner must be a qualifying first-time buyer who moves in first, and only that person claims. You can't claim if a corporation or partnership co-owns the home.

Which homes and dates count

  • New or substantially renovated homes bought from a builder: the purchase agreement must be signed on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031, construction must start before 2031, and the home must be finished and ownership transferred before 2036.
  • Homes you build yourself (or hire someone to build): construction must start on or after March 20, 2025 and before 2031.
  • Shares in a new housing co-op, plus some mobile, modular and floating homes.

Be careful with older deals. If a purchase agreement signed before March 20, 2025 is changed, assigned or cancelled and replaced to get the rebate, the law can treat it as the original, earlier agreement.

How much you get

  • Price of $1 million or less: all of the GST, up to $50,000.
  • Between $1 million and $1.5 million: a reduced rebate that shrinks evenly as the price rises. The CRA's example: a $1.25 million home gets $25,000.
  • $1.5 million or more: no FTHB rebate.

The regular GST new housing rebate (36% of the GST, up to $6,300, for homes under $450,000) still exists. You can claim the FTHB rebate on its own or with it, but the two together can't go over $50,000.

What to do now

  • Buying from a builder: ask whether the builder will credit the rebate on your closing statement. You sign Form GST190, usually with your lawyer or notary. If the builder doesn't credit it, apply yourself through CRA My Account ("File a GST/HST rebate") or by mailing Form GST190.
  • Already closed? If you closed before March 12, 2026, or the builder only credited the regular rebate, you can still send in a separate FTHB application.
  • Owner-built homes: apply online or with Forms GST191 and GST191-WS.
  • Watch the deadline: generally two years from the date ownership transfers to you (for owner-built homes, generally two years after the home is substantially finished).
  • Expect a wait: the CRA says applications take about 120 days to process, longer if reviewed.
  • Keep records for six years: the purchase agreement, statement of adjustments, invoices, your application and proof you moved in. If you get a rebate you weren't entitled to, you must pay it back.

B.C. property transfer tax breaks

Property transfer tax is a separate provincial tax, with its own exemptions:

  • Newly built home exemption: no property transfer tax on a new home worth less than $1.1 million that you will live in, with a partial exemption up to $1.15 million. You don't have to be a first-time buyer.
  • First-time home buyers' exemption: up to $8,000 off for homes worth up to $835,000, with a smaller amount up to $860,000.

Both require you to move in within 92 days and live there for the first year. The B.C. pages don't say you can combine the two, so check with the province which one fits your purchase.
