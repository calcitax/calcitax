Grocery benefit boost for early 2027 passes the House; the Senate is next

By Calcitax Advisors · November 28, 2026

A bill that would give more than 12 million people a one-time extra payment of the Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit in early 2027 has cleared the House of Commons and is now before the Senate. The same bill would also keep the federal ban on foreign buyers of existing homes in place for two more years. It is not law yet.

What happened

When the federal budget came out on November 5, 2026, it promised a one-time grocery benefit supplement (see our Budget 2026 summary). What's new is that the promise now has a bill behind it, and the bill is moving quickly:

  • November 16, 2026: Finance Canada announced the legislation.
  • November 18: Bill C-42, the Grocery and Housing Affordability Act, was introduced in the House of Commons.
  • November 20: the House passed it, with second reading, committee study and third reading all on the same day.
  • November 24: the bill had its first reading in the Senate.
  • December 3: senators are scheduled to question the Finance Minister and the Housing Minister about the bill in a Committee of the Whole.

The bill becomes law only after the Senate passes it and it receives Royal Assent. Changes are still possible.

What the grocery supplement would be

The Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit (CGEB) is the quarterly payment that took over from the GST/HST credit in July 2026. It goes to people with low and modest incomes. Bill C-42 would raise the maximum benefit for the July 2026 to June 2027 benefit year by 50%, paid as a one-time supplement in early 2027.

  • Current maximums (July 2026 to June 2027): up to $679 for a single person, $890 for a married or common-law couple, plus $234 for each child under 19.
  • Proposed supplement: up to $339 for a single person with no children and up to $679 for a couple with two children, according to Finance Canada.
  • Who would get it: people who already get the CGEB. Finance Canada says over 12 million individuals and families. You get less as your income goes up, so many people will receive less than the maximum.
  • Timing: "early 2027". No payment date has been announced yet.

What it means for you

If you already get the CGEB, you shouldn't need to do anything extra. The CRA says you don't apply for the benefit itself. It works it out from your tax return, so the key step is filing a return every year, even if you had no income. Your 2025 return sets your payments from July 2026 to June 2027, so if you haven't filed it, the CRA has nothing to base your payments on.

Newcomers are the main exception. In your first year as a resident, you may need to apply using Form RC151. Our newcomers tax guide and family benefits guide explain how.

The foreign-buyer ban on existing homes

Bill C-42 would also keep the Prohibition on the Purchase of Residential Property by Non-Canadians Act in force until January 1, 2029. The ban began on January 1, 2023 and is currently due to end on January 1, 2027. Finance Canada says it will also start a regulatory process to exempt new residential properties. That exemption would come through regulations, not this bill, and the details (including what counts as a new home) have not been published.

If you are a non-Canadian thinking about buying a home, or you are selling to one, get legal advice before you sign anything. The rules for new homes may change during 2027.

What to do now

  • Check that your 2025 tax return has been filed and assessed. If you are behind, file now.
  • Log in to CRA My Account and make sure your address, direct deposit and marital status are up to date.
  • Don't spend the supplement until the bill passes and the CRA announces a payment date.
  • If a regular CGEB payment is late, the CRA asks you to wait 10 business days before calling.

We will post an update once the Senate votes and a payment date is confirmed.
