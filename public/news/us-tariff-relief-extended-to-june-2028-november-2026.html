Ottawa extends relief from counter-tariffs on U.S. goods to June 2028, with some cuts

By Calcitax Advisors · November 12, 2026

On November 9, 2026, the Department of Finance Canada announced that relief from Canada's counter-tariffs on many goods imported from the United States will continue for another year, until June 30, 2028. At the same time, the relief is being widened for some uses and removed for a few products that are also made in Canada.

What happened

Since 2025, Canada has charged extra duties, often called surtaxes or counter-tariffs, on a range of U.S. goods in response to U.S. tariffs. On September 8, 2026, Canada added further counter-tariffs of 15% to 50% on more U.S. products.

To soften the blow for Canadian businesses that rely on U.S. inputs, the government offers remission, which means the surtax is waived or refunded. The broadest form is called horizontal remission: it applies to every importer, as long as the goods are used for certain purposes. It started on April 16, 2025, and has been extended and changed several times since.

The November 9 changes are:

  • One more year. Horizontal remission now runs to June 30, 2028.
  • New eligible uses. Non-steel goods used in mining, quarrying or oil and gas extraction now qualify. All goods used by public education bodies now qualify too, including those mandated by Indigenous organizations.
  • Relief ending for some products made in Canada. Certain trailers lose the relief on December 1, 2026. Certain aluminum wire and extrusions, and certain paperboard products, lose it on February 1, 2027. The government says the lead time is meant to let importers find other suppliers or apply for case-by-case relief.
  • Targeted relief. Some goods in short supply in Canada, and some companies facing exceptional circumstances (such as contracts signed before the tariffs), are getting specific relief.
  • Longer time to claim. Importers now have four years after the date of importation to make a remission claim, instead of two.
  • Clearer product lists. Detailed product schedules will be posted on the Finance Canada website.

Which goods qualify

Under the current rules, horizontal relief covers U.S. goods imported for these end uses:

  • goods used by certain public health, public safety, national security or public education organizations;
  • non-steel goods used for manufacturing or processing, farming, packaging farm products, packaging food or drinks as part of manufacturing, or mining, quarrying and oil and gas extraction.

Products listed in Schedule 7 of the government's remission list stop being eligible on the expiry date shown there. That is how the trailer, aluminum and paperboard changes take effect.

What it means for small businesses

If you import from the U.S. and the goods go into something you make, grow or package, this relief can make a real difference to your costs. A few points to keep in mind:

  • You don't apply to Finance Canada for horizontal relief. Instead, you must keep records showing the goods were actually used for an eligible purpose. Keep them ready in case the CBSA or Finance Canada asks.
  • Resale isn't a listed use. Goods bought simply to resell, such as stock for a store, are not among the eligible uses listed above.
  • Check your product list. If you buy trailers, aluminum wire or extrusions, or paperboard from U.S. suppliers, check whether your items are on the list losing relief, and plan for higher landed costs from December 1, 2026 or February 1, 2027.
  • Budget for it. Higher import costs can squeeze margins. The Bank of Canada has noted that businesses plan to pass on more costs over the next year, as we covered in our November 11 article.

What it means for you

For most households, there is nothing to do. The changes mainly affect businesses that import. Over time, removing relief on some products could nudge prices up for goods made with them, but how much, if at all, will depend on suppliers.

What to do now

  • Review your 2025 and 2026 U.S. imports and how each item was used. If you paid surtax on goods that qualified, you now have up to four years from the import date to claim it back.
  • Keep invoices, customs records and proof of how the goods were used (for example, production or job records) in one place.
  • Talk to your customs broker about the product schedules and any special codes needed on your customs declarations.
  • If a product you need is hard to get in Canada, you can ask Finance Canada for case-by-case remission. Its tariff relief page links to the request process.
  • Record surtax paid and refunds received correctly in your books, so your cost of goods and GST records line up. Our GST/HST and PST guide covers the sales tax side.

Tariff rules have changed often since 2025 and may change again, so check the Finance Canada pages below before making major purchasing decisions.
