Bank of Canada says more rate hikes may be needed: what it means for borrowers

By Calcitax Advisors · November 11, 2026

On November 10, 2026, the Bank of Canada published a summary of the discussions behind its October rate increase. The main message: its leaders believe further increases could be needed, but they will decide one meeting at a time. Separately, on November 5 the Bank and the federal government renewed the Bank's 2% inflation target for another five years.

What happened

On October 28, 2026, the Bank raised its key interest rate from 2.25% to 2.5%, its first increase since 2023. We covered that decision in our earlier article. About two weeks after each decision, the Bank releases a "summary of deliberations", a plain account of what its six-member Governing Council weighed before deciding. This is the new information.

According to the summary:

  • The council considered holding rates steady. Members who leaned that way pointed out that inflation was concentrated in energy and energy-heavy services, that core inflation measures were near 2%, and that new US tariffs would slow the economy.
  • The case for a hike won out. Oil prices were about US$35 a barrel higher than the Bank assumed in July, and markets expect them to stay high for longer. The council judged that the risk of high energy costs spreading into broad, lasting inflation had grown.
  • Inflation is expected to stay above target for a while. Consumer prices have been rising at around 3% a year since April. The Bank expects inflation to reach about 3.5% by the end of 2026, stay above 3% for much of 2027 and return to 2% by mid-2028.
  • Businesses expect to pass on more costs. So far, most higher costs (mainly fuel, but also some imported materials) have not reached store shelves. The Bank's surveys suggest businesses plan to pass on a larger share over the next year.
  • The economy has held up. Growth was 3.3% (annual pace) in the second quarter and about 2% in the third. Unemployment has been around 6.5%. The Bank expects growth of about 1% for 2026, then about 2% in each of 2027 and 2028, although trade tension with the US is a risk.

The Bank's position, in its own words, is that it will not let higher energy prices turn into "broad-based, persistent inflation".

The 2% target stays

The Bank and the Government of Canada renew their inflation agreement every five years. The new agreement, announced on November 5, 2026, keeps the target at 2%, in the middle of a 1% to 3% range, and runs from January 2027 to December 2031. In practice, this means the Bank will keep using interest rates to pull inflation back toward 2%, which is why it is willing to raise rates now.

What it means for you

  • Variable-rate mortgages and lines of credit. These usually follow the Bank's rate through your lender's prime rate. Another increase would raise your interest cost again. If your budget is already tight, plan now for a possible further 0.25 percentage point rise.
  • Fixed-rate mortgages. Nothing changes until renewal. If you renew in the next year, start comparing options early and ask your lender or broker how a higher rate would change your payment.
  • Savers. Rates on savings accounts and GICs tend to rise when the Bank raises rates, so it may be worth checking what your bank is paying.
  • Household budgets. With inflation expected to stay above 3% for much of 2027, prices for fuel and travel in particular may keep rising faster than usual.

What it means for small businesses

Floating-rate business loans and lines of credit could cost more if the Bank raises again. It is a good time to review your cash flow forecast for early 2027, pay down expensive debt where you can, and think about whether higher fuel and supply costs need to show up in your own prices. If you are planning a larger purchase of equipment, talk to your lender about locking in a rate.

What to do now

  1. Check the rate and renewal date on your mortgage and any lines of credit.
  2. Work out what a further 0.25 percentage point increase would do to your monthly payment.
  3. Shop around for better interest on your savings.
  4. Keep an eye on the next rate decision on December 9, 2026. We will explain what it means once it is announced.

The Bank stresses that its outlook is uncertain, citing the war in the Middle East, Canada-US trade relations and global financial conditions. No further increase is certain, and nobody can say for sure where rates will go.
