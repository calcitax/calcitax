Federal Budget 2026: grocery benefit top-up, refundable caregiver credit and more

By Calcitax Advisors · November 6, 2026

The federal government tabled Budget 2026 on November 5, 2026. It does not change personal or corporate income tax rates, but it proposes a one-time boost to the grocery benefit, a refundable caregiver credit, a bigger workers benefit, a new GST/HST exemption for massage therapy and a plan to drop the age-71 RRSP deadline. Here is what matters for families and small businesses in Metro Vancouver.

What happened

The government projects a deficit of $62.3 billion for 2026-27, falling to $52.9 billion by 2030-31. Remember that a budget is a plan: most of the tax changes below are proposed and only become law once Parliament passes the legislation. Some start right away; others start in 2027.

What it means for you

  • Extra grocery benefit payment in early 2027. The Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit gets a one-time supplement equal to a 50% top-up of the 2026-27 benefit. Finance Canada says this means up to $339 for a single person without children and up to $679 for a couple with two children. You don't apply: it is based on your tax return, so make sure you have filed.
  • Caregiver credit becomes refundable. The Canada Caregiver Credit, for people who support a family member with a physical or mental impairment, would become refundable starting with the 2027 tax year. "Refundable" means you get the full value even if you owe little or no tax. Finance Canada puts the benefit at up to $1,260 a year.
  • Bigger benefit for lower-income workers. The Canada Workers Benefit would be renamed the Build Canada Workers Benefit from 2027. The maximum would rise from $1,709 to $1,859 for single people without children and from $2,943 to $3,143 for families, and you would start qualifying once you earn $500 (down from $3,000). The trade-off: advance payments shrink to 25% of the 2026 amount in 2027 and end in 2028, so more of the benefit would arrive when you file.
  • An "RRSP for life". Today an RRSP has to be wound up by the end of the year you turn 71, usually by converting it to a RRIF. The government wants to combine the two so there is no forced conversion at 71, though minimum withdrawals would still apply. This is only a consultation (submissions are due January 8, 2027), so nothing changes yet. If you or a parent turn 71 this year, the current deadline still applies; see our article on the turning-71 RRSP deadline and our retirement income guide.
  • More time to object. If you disagree with a CRA assessment, the deadline to file an objection would become one year after the notice of assessment, or two years from your filing due date if that is later. This would apply once the law passes.
  • Volunteer firefighters and search and rescue. The two separate 14% credits would merge into one from 2027, and hours from both kinds of service would count toward the 200-hour minimum.

What it means for small businesses

  • Massage therapy exempt from GST/HST. Services by qualifying massage therapists (licensed or certified where the province requires it) become exempt for supplies made after November 5, 2026. If you are a registered massage therapist, talk to your accountant soon about how this affects your invoices, your GST/HST registration and the input tax credits you claim on business costs. Our GST and PST guide explains how this works.
  • Tax debts and your credit report. The CRA would be allowed to report income tax or GST/HST debts over $50,000 to credit bureaus, but only once all objection and appeal rights are used up or expired, and only after 30 days' written notice and a chance to set up a payment plan.
  • Canada Growth Incentive. Individuals who invest in qualifying new shares of Canadian businesses issued on or after November 5, 2026 could shelter up to $15 million of capital gains, with holding-period rules and many excluded sectors (including professional practices, real estate and food and accommodation). A related rollover for reinvested small-business gains is extended from January 1, 2027.
  • Artists. Employed artists could deduct expenses up to the full amount of their artistic employment income (instead of 20%) from 2027.

What to do now

  • File any missing tax returns so you receive the early-2027 grocery benefit supplement and other benefits automatically.
  • If you care for a family member, keep records now: the refundable credit would apply to your 2027 return.
  • Massage therapists should review their GST/HST registration and invoicing from November 6, 2026 onward.
  • Don't change your year-end plans on the strength of proposals alone. We will report when these measures become law.
