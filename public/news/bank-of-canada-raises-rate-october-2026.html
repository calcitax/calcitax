Bank of Canada raises its rate to 2.5%: what it means for you

By Calcitax Advisors · October 31, 2026

On October 28, 2026, the Bank of Canada raised its key interest rate by a quarter of a percentage point, from 2.25% to 2.5%. It's the Bank's first rate increase since July 2023, and it signals that the long run of rate cuts is over for now.

Why the Bank raised rates

Inflation has been higher and has lasted longer than the Bank expected in the summer. Consumer prices have been rising at around 3% a year since April, and the Bank now expects inflation to reach about 3.5% by the end of 2026. High energy prices are the main driver. When gasoline is left out, inflation was a much lower 2.2% in September.

The Bank's concern is that expensive fuel will spread into other prices, as businesses pass on higher transport and production costs. Raising rates now is meant to stop that from turning into a longer bout of inflation.

The economy has also been stronger than expected. It grew at a 3.3% annual pace in the second quarter, and early estimates suggest about 2% in the third. The Bank expects growth of about 1% for 2026 as a whole, with new US tariffs likely to slow things down in the last few months of the year. Unemployment has been around 6.5% for several months.

What it means for you

  • Variable-rate mortgages and lines of credit. Lenders usually move their prime rates in step with the Bank's rate, so borrowing tied to prime generally gets more expensive. Check your lender's notice to see how your payment changes.
  • Fixed-rate mortgages. Your payment won't change until renewal. Fixed rates follow bond yields rather than the Bank's rate directly, and bond yields barely moved on the news.
  • Renewing soon? Compare fixed and variable options carefully, since the Bank has said further increases are possible.
  • Savers. Rates on high-interest savings accounts and GICs may edge up.

What it means for small businesses

Business loans and lines of credit at floating rates will cost a little more. If you carry a balance on a business line of credit, now is a good time to look at your cash flow, pay down expensive debt where you can, and think about how you would handle another increase.

What's next

The Bank said it may need to raise rates further, but will move carefully given the uncertainty around energy prices and trade. Economists at TD expect one more increase in early 2027, which would take the rate to 2.75%. Forecasts like this can change quickly.

The next decision is on December 9, 2026. The Bank's next full economic forecast, the Monetary Policy Report, follows on January 27, 2027.
