CRA updates for small businesses: online debt payments, Apple Pay and more

By Calcitax Advisors · October 31, 2026

The Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) has rolled out several changes this fall that make dealing with it easier for business owners. Here are the ones most likely to matter to small businesses, contractors and corporations.

Manage business tax debts online

Owners, directors and officers can now deal with payroll, GST/HST and corporate (T2) tax balances directly in My Business Account. The new Manage Balance service lets you make a payment, set up a payment arrangement or ask the CRA to call you back, without waiting on the phone. A similar service for personal tax debts launched in October 2025.

One limit to know: accountants and other representatives can't use this service through Represent a Client, so the business owner or a director needs to sign in.

New ways to pay the CRA

Since October 9, 2026, you can pay the CRA with Apple Pay, Google Pay or Konek, as well as by debit card or Interac through participating financial institutions. Whichever way you pay, keep the confirmation with your records.

Keep your director and officer details current

The CRA is reminding corporations to update their records whenever a director or officer joins, leaves or changes their details. Out-of-date information can delay access to My Business Account and stop new representative authorizations from going through.

Rideshare and taxi drivers must register for GST/HST

If you drive for Uber, Lyft or a taxi company, you're generally a self-employed sole proprietor. The CRA's reminder is that rideshare and taxi operators must register for a Business Number and a GST/HST account no matter how much they earn. The usual $30,000 small-supplier threshold doesn't apply to them.

Other changes worth knowing

  • T2 web access code online. Corporations can now get the access code needed to file a T2 return electronically online, without waiting.
  • Faster SR&ED answers. Businesses doing research and development can apply for pre-claim approval, with a decision within eight weeks.
  • Free one-on-one help. The CRA's Liaison Officer Service offers free, confidential visits and sessions for small business owners, self-employed people and landlords.
  • 2027 deadlines. The CRA has published a factsheet of 2027 deadlines for businesses and the self-employed.
  • Extra sign-in security. Individuals and tax preparers can add an optional verification step when filing returns online this coming tax season.

What to do now

  • Make sure you can sign in to My Business Account, and that the right people have access.
  • Check that your directors, officers and addresses are up to date with the CRA.
  • If you owe the CRA and can't pay in full, set up a payment arrangement early. Interest keeps adding up on unpaid balances.
  • Drive for a rideshare platform? Make sure you're registered for GST/HST and charging and remitting it correctly.
