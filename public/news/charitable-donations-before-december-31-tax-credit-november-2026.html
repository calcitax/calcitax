Giving to charity this year? Donate by December 31 to claim it on your 2026 return

By Calcitax Advisors · November 21, 2026

Many people do most of their giving in late November and December. If you want a donation to reduce your 2026 taxes, it has to be made by December 31, 2026. Here is how the donation tax credit works in British Columbia this year, and a few simple ways to get more back from the same gift.

What happened

Nothing about the basic rules has changed, but two things are different for the 2026 tax year. The lowest federal tax rate dropped to 14%, and the lowest B.C. tax rate rose to 5.60% (it was 5.06% for 2025). Because the credit on the first $200 of donations is tied to these lowest rates, the credit on that first slice changes a little. The federal budget tabled on November 5, 2026 did not propose any change to the donation credit for individual donors; its charity measures are aimed at charities and foundations themselves.

How the donation credit works

A donation to a registered charity (or another "qualified donee" the CRA recognizes) gives you a non-refundable tax credit. That means it lowers the tax you owe, but it can't create a refund on its own if you owe no tax.

The credit has two tiers. Your first $200 of donations in the year gets a lower rate. Everything above $200 gets a much higher rate. For 2026, the rates are:

  • Federal: 14% on the first $200, then 29% on the rest. The rate rises to 33% on the part of your donations that matches income taxed in the top federal bracket.
  • B.C.: 5.60% on the first $200, then 16.8% on the rest. The B.C. rate rises to 20.5% on the part that matches taxable income above $265,545.

A simple example (an illustration only): you live in B.C., you're not in a top tax bracket, and you give $1,000 in 2026. Your federal credit is $28 on the first $200 plus $232 on the next $800, or $260. Your B.C. credit is $11.20 plus $134.40, or $145.60. Altogether, the gift cuts your tax by about $405.60.

What it means for you

Because amounts over $200 earn a much bigger credit, the way you group donations matters:

  • Combine with your spouse or partner. Donations made by both spouses or common-law partners can be added together and claimed by either one. Claiming them all on one return means the $200 low-rate slice only applies once.
  • Carry donations forward. You don't have to claim a donation in the year you make it. Unclaimed donations can be carried forward and claimed in any of the next five years. If you gave only a small amount in 2026, holding it over and adding it to next year's gifts can push more of the total above $200.
  • Know the limit. In most years you can claim donations up to 75% of your net income. In the year of death, the limit is 100% of net income, which matters for estate planning.
  • Consider giving shares instead of cash. If you own shares or units listed on a designated stock exchange that have gone up in value, donating them directly to the charity (rather than selling first) can mean no tax on the capital gain, and you still get a receipt for their fair market value. Talk to your broker and the charity early, because transfers take time.

What to do now

  • Give by December 31. The donation date is the date the gift is made, so a gift made in January counts for 2027, not 2026. Online and mailed gifts can take time to process, so don't leave it to the last day.
  • Check the charity is registered. Use the CRA's "Find a charity" search before you give. Only gifts to registered charities and other qualified donees qualify.
  • Ask for an official receipt. Without a valid official receipt you can't claim the credit. Charities don't always send one automatically, so ask. You can usually expect it by February 28 of the next year. Gifts of goods or shares need their own receipt for each donation.
  • Keep your records. If you file online, keep official receipts for five years in case the CRA asks to see them, along with proof of payment such as card slips or bank statements.
  • Don't forget donations through work. Gifts made through payroll deductions may already appear on your T4 slip, and the slip can serve as your receipt.
