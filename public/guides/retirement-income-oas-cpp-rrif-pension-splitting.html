Retirement income in Canada: OAS, CPP, RRIFs and pension splitting

OAS, CPP and your RRSP or RRIF savings each have their own rules on timing and tax. Here's how they work in 2026, what is known for 2027, how to limit the OAS clawback, what to do with your RRSP at 71, and how couples can split pension income.

By Calcitax Advisors · Updated November 28, 2026 · Figures for 2026 (OAS and GIS: October to December 2026), plus the 2027 OAS clawback threshold and age amount

In short

  • Maximum OAS for October to December 2026: $762.50 a month at 65 to 74 and $838.75 at 75+. It rises 0.4% for January to March 2027.
  • OAS is clawed back at 15% of net income over $95,323 for 2026 and $97,801 for 2027.
  • CPP can start from 60 to 70: 0.6% less per month before 65, 0.7% more per month after 65.
  • By the end of the year you turn 71, your RRSP must become a RRIF, an annuity or cash. Budget 2026 proposes merging RRSPs and RRIFs, but that isn't law yet.
  • Couples can split up to 50% of eligible pension income on Form T1032, but not CPP or OAS.

The main pieces of retirement income

Most retirees live on a mix of government pensions and their own savings:

  • Old Age Security (OAS): a monthly federal pension for people 65 and older. It is based on how long you have lived in Canada, not on your work.
  • Guaranteed Income Supplement (GIS): an extra monthly payment on top of OAS for seniors with low income.
  • Canada Pension Plan (CPP): a monthly pension based on what you paid into CPP while working.
  • Registered retirement income fund (RRIF): an account that pays your savings back to you as income, usually after you move them from your registered retirement savings plan (RRSP).
  • Workplace pensions: payments from a pension plan at work.

OAS, CPP, RRIF payments and workplace pensions are all taxable. GIS is not. Figures below are for 2026 (OAS and GIS: October to December 2026), plus 2027 figures already posted.

Old Age Security and the Guaranteed Income Supplement

Who can get OAS

To get OAS while living in Canada, you must be 65 or older, a citizen or legal resident when your application is approved, and have lived here for at least 10 years since turning 18. If you live outside Canada, you need at least 20 years of residence in Canada after age 18.

A full pension is based on 40 years in Canada after age 18. With fewer years, you get a partial pension: your years in Canada divided by 40.

How much you can get (October to December 2026)

PaymentMaximum per month
OAS, age 65 to 74$762.50
OAS, age 75 and older$838.75
GIS, single person (yearly income under $23,112, not counting OAS)$1,138.90

The higher amount at 75 comes from a permanent 10% increase that starts the month after your 75th birthday. OAS is reviewed every January, April, July and October for the cost of living, and never goes down when prices fall. Payments went up 1.4% for October to December 2026, and Service Canada says they will rise another 0.4% for January to March 2027. The new dollar amounts weren't posted yet when we checked.

You can also delay OAS for up to 5 years after 65. Each month you wait adds 0.6%, up to 36% more if you start at 70.

GIS and the B.C. Senior's Supplement

GIS depends on your income and your spouse's income. It is reviewed every year using your tax return, and Service Canada sends a letter in July with your new amount. File your return by April 30 each year so your payments don't stop.

In B.C., low-income seniors who get OAS and GIS may also get the Senior's Supplement from the province: up to $99.30 a month for a single senior and up to $220.50 a month for a couple. You don't need to apply; it is paid automatically to people who qualify.

The OAS clawback (recovery tax)

If your income is high, you may have to pay back some or all of your OAS. This is the OAS pension recovery tax, often called the clawback.

  • The clawback starts when your individual net income is over $95,323 for 2026. For 2027, the CRA has set the threshold at $97,801. (For 2025 income, it was $93,454.)
  • You repay 15% of your income above that threshold.
  • Service Canada's table for 2026 income shows OAS fully clawed back at $155,320 of income if you are 65 to 74, and $161,320 if you are 75 or older.

Net income is the amount on line 23600 of your tax return: your total income minus deductions such as RRSP contributions. It includes OAS, CPP, pensions, RRIF withdrawals and investment income.

Illustration only: $10,000 over the 2026 threshold means repaying 15% of $10,000, or $1,500.

The repayment comes off future OAS payments over 12 months. For example, the amount based on 2026 income comes off payments from July 2027 to June 2028.

Tip: Because the clawback looks at your own net income, not your family's, pension income splitting (explained below) can sometimes lower or remove it. A big one-time RRIF or RRSP withdrawal can do the opposite. Also note that OAS and CPP are taxable, but tax is not taken off them unless you ask Service Canada to do so.

The Canada Pension Plan: when to start

You can start your CPP retirement pension any time from age 60 to age 70. The age you pick changes your monthly amount for life:

If you startChange to your monthly pension
Before 65Goes down 0.6% for each month before 65 (up to 36% less at 60)
At 65Your standard amount
After 65Goes up 0.7% for each month after 65 (up to 42% more at 70)

There is no gain from waiting past 70.

For a pension starting at 65 in 2026, the most anyone could get is $1,507.65 a month. The 2027 maximum had not been posted when we checked. Most people get less, because it depends on what you contributed. The average for new retirees starting at 65 was $858.34 a month in July 2026. My Service Canada Account shows your own estimate.

Early means more, smaller payments; late means fewer, bigger ones. Your health, savings and work plans all matter.

CPP pension sharing

CPP has its own way to share income with a spouse or common-law partner, called pension sharing. If you live together and at least one of you gets (or has applied for) a CPP retirement pension, you can ask Service Canada to split it between you. This may lower your tax. Apply in My Service Canada Account or on form ISP1002. It is separate from the tax-return splitting below, which can't include CPP.

CPP is reported on your return from your T4A(P) slip.

From RRSP to RRIF: the age-71 deadline and minimum withdrawals

Under the current rules, in the year you turn 71 you must choose what to do with your RRSP savings by the end of that year:

  • cash it out (all of it is taxed as income that year, which can be costly);
  • move it to a RRIF; or
  • buy an annuity, a contract that pays you a set income, often for life.

No tax is withheld on a direct move into a RRIF or an annuity.

Minimum withdrawals

A RRIF must pay you at least a minimum amount every year, starting the year after you open it. The minimum for the first year is zero. Each year, the minimum is the RRIF's value on January 1 multiplied by a percentage set by your age at the start of the year:

  • Under 71: 1 divided by (90 minus your age). At 65, that is 1/25, or 4%.
  • 71 and older: a fixed percentage from the CRA's table, for example 5.28% at 71, 5.82% at 75, 6.82% at 80, 8.51% at 85, 11.92% at 90 and 20% at 95 or older.

Illustration only: a $200,000 RRIF on January 1, for someone 71 on that date, must pay at least $200,000 × 5.28% = $10,560 that year.

If your spouse or common-law partner is younger, you can base the minimum on their age instead, for a lower minimum. You must make this choice before you receive any payments from that RRIF, and you can't change it later.

Watch the tax: Your RRIF carrier doesn't withhold tax on the minimum amount, but it must withhold tax on anything you take above it. Withholding is only a prepayment. Cash taken from an RRSP has 10% withheld on amounts up to $5,000, 20% from $5,000 to $15,000 and 30% over $15,000 (outside Quebec), often less than the tax you really owe.

What Budget 2026 proposes for retirees

The federal budget tabled on November 5, 2026 includes ideas that would affect retirees. These are proposals, not law yet, and they could change, so follow today's rules for now.

  • An "RRSP for life": the government intends to merge the main features of RRSPs and RRIFs, which could mean no longer having to switch your RRSP to a RRIF at 71. People over 71 would still have to take minimum withdrawals. No start date is set; Finance Canada is taking public comments until January 8, 2027.
  • A refundable Canada caregiver credit: caregivers with little or no tax to pay could get the full credit, starting with the 2027 tax year.
  • A later tax deadline: the filing and payment date for individuals would move to May 15, starting with 2027 returns. Until then, plan on April 30.

Bill C-43, the first bill to carry out the budget, was introduced on November 26, 2026. Before you act on any of these ideas, check that the measure has actually become law.

Pension income splitting, the pension income amount and the age amount

Pension income splitting

Tax rates rise as income goes up, so a couple usually pays less tax when their incomes are close together. Pension income splitting lets you move up to 50% of your eligible pension income to your spouse or common-law partner on your tax returns. No money changes hands.

What counts as eligible pension income depends on the age of the person transferring it:

  • Any age: the taxable part of lifetime payments from a workplace pension plan.
  • 65 or older at the end of the year: also RRIF payments (including payments from a life income fund, or LIF, which is a special kind of RRIF) and annuity payments from an RRSP.
  • Never eligible: OAS, CPP, and U.S. individual retirement account (IRA) income.

To split, you and your partner both fill out and sign Form T1032, Joint Election to Split Pension Income, and each of you files it with your own return by the filing deadline. The forms must match, and tax withheld on the pension moves with it in the same proportion. The percentage can change each year.

You can't split if, at year end, you had lived apart for 90 days or more because your relationship broke down. Both of you must be Canadian residents on December 31. If you forgot to split in an earlier year, in some cases you can ask the CRA to accept a late election within three years after that year's filing due date.

Illustration only: Asha, 68, has a $40,000 workplace pension and her husband has $10,000 of income. Moving $15,000 to him leaves them about $25,000 each, which often means less tax in total.

The pension income amount

The pension income amount is a federal tax credit on up to $2,000 of eligible pension income a year (the CRA's figure for 2025). Splitting even a small amount can let a spouse with no pension income claim it too.

The age amount

If you are 65 or older on December 31, you can claim the age amount, a federal tax credit. For 2026, it is up to $9,208 and starts to shrink once your net income is over $46,432. For 2027, the CRA has set it at up to $9,448, shrinking above $47,639. If you can't use all of it, you may be able to transfer some to your spouse or common-law partner. Splitting changes each spouse's net income, so it can also change how much age amount each keeps. See also our guide to often-missed tax credits.

Checklist and common mistakes

Checklist

  • Checked your CPP estimate in My Service Canada Account and thought about when to start (60 to 70).
  • Applied for OAS, or chosen to delay it, around 65.
  • If your income is low, made sure you are getting GIS (and the B.C. Senior's Supplement follows automatically).
  • Planned your RRSP's next step before December 31 of the year you turn 71.
  • Arranged enough tax withholding on RRIF, pension, CPP and OAS payments to avoid a surprise bill.
  • Filed Form T1032 with both returns if splitting pension income.

Common mistakes

  • Trying to split CPP or OAS on the tax return. CPP has its own pension sharing; OAS can't be split.
  • Splitting RRIF income when the person transferring it is under 65.
  • Triggering the OAS clawback with one big RRIF or RRSP withdrawal.
  • Acting on a budget proposal, such as the "RRSP for life", before it becomes law.
  • Forgetting about foreign pensions. A pension from India or elsewhere usually goes on your Canadian return. If you are thinking of moving abroad in retirement, see our leaving Canada tax guide.
